You've decided on a grout-free shower for the hall bath, and now the consultant is coming Thursday and the [colors page](/inspiration-colors-patterns/) has thirty color swatches on it. Linda likes the gray one this week. Tom wants whatever won't look dated before the grandkids finish high school. Picking shower wall colors for the next twenty years comes down to a few things you can check in your own bathroom before anyone shows up, and most of them start with what you're not replacing.

We walk through eight of them at the kitchen table, in this order.

## 1. Start with the floor, vanity and toilet you're keeping

The wall is the biggest surface in the room, and it's the one you're choosing last. The floor, the vanity top and the toilet are already there, and in a lot of the 1960s and 70s ranches we work in, at least one of them is almond, biscuit or a cultured marble with a gold vein through it.

[Our one-day job](/one-day-bathroom-remodel/) covers the wet area: the crew takes the tub or shower area to the studs and rebuilds it with a new base, walls and fixtures in a day, and you take your first shower the next morning. The floor, vanity and toilet are a separate, longer job. Plenty of homeowners [keep them for now](/blog/replace-shower-keep-vanity-and-floor/), so the new wall has to get along with them.

Take a phone photo of the floor and the vanity top in daylight and have it out when the consultant opens the sample case. Then hold each sample against the real floor and the real top.

| What you're keeping | Wall looks to pull samples of | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Almond or biscuit toilet or floor | Almond, Biscuit, Bianco Travertine, Horizon Beige | Bright white walls make almond look yellow |
| White fixtures, gray vinyl floor | Calcutta Marble, Santorini White Marble, Silver White, London Fog | Warm beige stone can look dingy next to cool gray |
| Cultured marble top with gold or tan veins | Travertine, Tuscany, Sandbar | Match the vein color, not the background |
| Oak vanity | Bianco Travertine, Sandalwood, Horizon Beige | Gray stone against orange oak reads as two different houses |

## 2. Light walls in a small hall bath

The hall bath in an Anderson Township ranch is small, with one fixture over the mirror and often no window. Every bit of light in there bounces off the walls. Paint makers rate colors by light reflectance value, a scale that [runs from 0 to 100, with zero reflecting no light and 100 reflecting all of it](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/project-center/paint/color-by-room-orientation), and Sherwin-Williams tells you to [look for higher numbers in north-facing rooms](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/project-center/paint/color-by-room-orientation). A shower wall works the same way. The wall goes floor to ceiling on three sides of the tub alcove, so its reflectance counts at least as much as the paint's.

Most of the colors on our [colors and patterns page](/inspiration-colors-patterns/) are light to begin with: white and gray marbles, cream travertines, solids like Arctic Ice and Glacier Ice. The darker, browner looks, such as Madeira / Santa Cruz Granite and River Rock / Canyon Rock, suit a master bath with a window better than a hall bath lit by one bulb.

## 3. The marble and travertine looks most of our customers pick

Most of our customers pick a marble or travertine look. They've been around through a lot of color trends, which is why we point people there first when the goal is twenty years. Calcutta Marble and Santorini White Marble are white with gray veining. Bianco Travertine and Travertine are the warmer, creamier option. Napoli Marble / Pompeii Marble sits in between.

These are marble-look and travertine-look patterns in a solid-surface panel. They aren't stone, and they don't need the sealing that stone does. If the worry in the back of your mind is a plastic insert, [our post on whether panel walls look like one](/blog/do-shower-wall-panels-look-cheap/) covers how to judge that from the doorway.

A rule of thumb for a couple in Montgomery or Fort Thomas, Kentucky, who can't agree: pick the travertine if the rest of the room is warm and the white marble if it's cool, and let the trim and the door hardware carry the personality.

## 4. How dark colors and hard water show on a shower wall

We don't have a lab test for our own panels on this, so we'll point you to the closest one we've found. The care guide for Corian, a solid-surface material made for countertops, says [dark, heavily pigmented colors show scratches, dust and ordinary wear more readily than lighter, textured colors](https://www.corian.com/-use-care-). The same guide says letting hard water dry on the surface [leaves behind a film of minerals that makes the finish look blotchy](https://www.corian.com/-use-care-).

It's written for a kitchen counter, so treat it as a guide and not a rule for our walls. If your water leaves white rings on the faucet, a light stone look with veining should show the spots less than a dark solid between Saturdays. [Our cleaning guide for grout-free showers](/blog/how-to-clean-grout-free-shower/) has the soap-and-water routine and the vinegar trick for the glass.

## 5. Matte or gloss on the solid colors

The solid colors, including White, come in matte or gloss, and you choose. We haven't found a source we trust that says one shows water spots more than the other, so we won't tell you it does. Ask the consultant to bring both, run a little water on each at your bathroom sink, and look at them dry an hour later. That test tells you more about your water and your light than any brochure.

## 6. Subway, herringbone and hexagonal patterns without grout

Some people grew up with tile and like the lines. The same panel comes in tile-look patterns: 3x6 Subway, 4x12 Subway, Herringbone, Hexagonal, Chevron and Picket, among others. The lines are part of one non-porous panel, so there's no grout to scrub, seal or regrout.

Of subway, herringbone and hexagonal, subway is the one we'd bet on for 2040, since it has stayed in bathrooms through several decades. Herringbone and Hexagonal carry more personality, and both read best on one wall, such as the back wall of the shower, with a plain stone look on the two sides.

Tile is a good product, and if what you want is a hand-set mosaic, a tile contractor is the right call. Bulldog doesn't sell tile. For a reader who wants the look of tile and is done scrubbing grout, the tile-look panel is the answer.

## 7. One busy thing per room

Count the busy surfaces you already have. A veined cultured marble top, a patterned vinyl floor and a busy wall all compete, and the room looks smaller for it. If the vanity top is busy, keep the wall calm: a white marble with light veining, or a solid. If the floor and vanity are plain, the wall can be the thing people notice, in Travertine or a herringbone pattern.

Accent trim and borders finish the edges, and recessed niches can be added. Pick those after the main color, and keep the trim in the same family.

## 8. Look at samples in your own light, then in 3D

A sample looks different under the showroom's ceiling lights than it does under the one fixture in your hall bath at 7 in the morning. Our design consultant brings samples to your house so you can see them in your own light. Tape your top two to the tub wall and leave them up for a day and a night.

Before anything is ordered, our designers can show you a 3D rendering of your bathroom with the color and pattern you picked. Ask for two versions side by side if you're split. It's a lot easier to change your mind on a screen than after the walls are fabricated.
