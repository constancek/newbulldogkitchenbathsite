The laminate in your West Chester kitchen went in around 1995, with the oak cabinets. There's a burn mark by the stove from a casserole dish, and the seam by the sink has started to lift. You've narrowed the replacement to quartz or granite, and someone mentioned solid surface. You may have searched quartz vs granite countertops, but the answer for a kitchen you'll cook in every day for 20 years comes down to six things: heat, stains, scratches, sealing, repairs and seams. Each material wins at least one of them.

## Quartz vs granite countertops, and solid surface, side by side

The three get mixed up because the names sound alike. Granite is stone, quarried and cut into slabs. Quartz is crushed quartz bound with resin into a slab at a factory. Solid surface is a sheet made of acrylic resin and minerals; the Corian version is [one-third acrylic resin and two-thirds natural minerals](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-27478_corian_solid_surface_product_overview-2021.pdf), according to DuPont's product bulletin. Quartz and solid surface both contain resin, and both makers ask for a trivet at the stove.

| | Quartz | Granite | Solid surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot pans | Trivet, per makers | Handles heat best, trivet still advised | Trivet, per makers |
| Stains and sealing | Nonporous, no sealing | Low absorption, most need little or no sealing | Nonporous, no sealing |
| Knives and scratches | Resists, use a board | Resists best | Shows scratches, use a board |
| Repairs | Chips are hard to blend, per stone industry | Chips easier to patch, per stone industry | Sand out scratches, on-site repair |
| Seams | Tinted epoxy, pattern hard to match | Tinted epoxy matched to the stone | Glued joints that are hard to see |

## Heat from the stove: granite wins

Granite is the one material of the three that comes from heat in the first place. The Natural Stone Institute, the stone industry's trade group, says granite is [heat resistant up to about 480°F](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/stoneprofessionals/varieties/granite/). The same page warns that direct, localized heat is discouraged, because a sharp difference in temperature across the stone can start a crack. Its consumer care page still says [trivets or mats are recommended](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/) on natural stone.

Quartz makers are firmer about it. DuPont's care sheet for Corian Quartz says the material is [heat- and scratch-resistant, but not heat- and scratch-proof](https://www.corianquartz.com/IMG/pdf/k27486_caring_for_corian_quartz.pdf), and to use trivets or pads with hot skillets and roasting pans. Caesarstone's US care page says [hot cookware should always go on trivets and hot pads](https://studio.caesarstoneus.com/care-and-maintenance/).

Solid surface gets the same rule, stated more bluntly. DuPont's care bulletin says [do not place hot pots or pans on the countertop](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-26840-corian-solid-surface-care-and-maintenance.pdf), and to keep a trivet under a toaster oven.

**Verdict: if the cast iron comes out of the oven and lands on the counter, granite forgives that mistake best. Keep a trivet by the stove whichever one you pick.**

## Stains and spills: quartz wins

Quartz has no pores for coffee or red wine to soak into. DuPont says Corian Quartz [resists permanent staining from wine, vinegar, tea, lemon juice and soda](https://www.corianquartz.com/IMG/pdf/k27486_caring_for_corian_quartz.pdf), and that it doesn't need sealants or waxes because it's nonporous. A damp cloth handles most days.

Granite comes close. The Natural Stone Institute puts granite's water absorption at [0.05 to 0.40 percent by weight](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/stoneprofessionals/varieties/granite/), which leaves little room for a stain to settle.

Solid surface is nonporous too, so spills sit on top. DuPont lists strong solvents, paint remover and acid drain cleaners as the things that [can stain or damage it](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-27478_corian_solid_surface_product_overview-2021.pdf). Its advantage shows up in the repair section below.

**Verdict: quartz, for the kitchen where the grandkids leave grape juice on the counter overnight.**

## Sealing granite: less than you've heard

Sealing is the worry that keeps a lot of people away from granite. The Natural Stone Institute's position is that [most granite countertops do not need to be sealed](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/sealing-stone/), though many benefit from it, and a good sealer can have a life expectancy of 10 to 15 years. Sealing makes stone [more stain resistant, not stain proof](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/).

A quick check tells you where yours stands: the stone group's consumer site says to put a little hot water on the stone, let it sit [10 to 20 minutes and wipe it off](https://usenaturalstone.org/caring-for-granite/), and if the stone has darkened, it's still absorbing moisture. Quartz and solid surface don't need sealing.

## Knives and scratches: granite wins

The minerals in most granite are harder than the knife. The Natural Stone Institute says that hardness [surpasses that of the utensils](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/stoneprofessionals/varieties/granite/), which gives granite excellent scratch resistance.

Quartz resists scratches but isn't scratch-proof, and DuPont says to [always use a cutting board](https://www.corianquartz.com/IMG/pdf/k27486_caring_for_corian_quartz.pdf) and never cut on the counter.

Solid surface needs the most care with knives. DuPont's care bulletin says [not to cut on it](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-26840-corian-solid-surface-care-and-maintenance.pdf), and the product bulletin notes that [dark, deep-pigment colors show scratches](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-27478_corian_solid_surface_product_overview-2021.pdf) and wear more than lighter, textured ones. A light solid-surface top hides more daily wear than a black one.

**Verdict: granite. With solid surface, pick a lighter, textured color, and keep the cutting board out on all three.**

## Repairs after 20 years: solid surface wins

This is where solid surface earns its place. The color runs through the whole sheet, so you can sand a scratch out. DuPont says owners can remove [minor cuts and scratches themselves](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-26840-corian-solid-surface-care-and-maintenance.pdf) with fine sandpaper and a Scotch-Brite pad, and that deeper damage is best left to a professional. The product bulletin says damage from abuse can [be repaired on site in most cases](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-27478_corian_solid_surface_product_overview-2021.pdf) without replacing the top.

Granite and quartz can both chip at an edge. The stone industry's consumer site, which promotes stone and says so, argues that a chip in quartz is [harder to repair than natural stone](https://usenaturalstone.org/granite-vs-engineered-quartz-countertop/) because the patch can't be polished to blend with the resin.

**Verdict: solid surface. It's the one of the three whose maker says you can sand out minor scratches yourself.**

## Seams: solid surface wins

Granite and quartz come in slabs with [size limits for each piece](https://www.arizonatile.com/?p=22689), so a long run or an L-shaped counter gets a seam. The installer fills it with epoxy tinted to the main color of the slab, according to [the same distributor's guide](https://www.arizonatile.com/?p=22689). On granite, it says matching the epoxy to the stone and cutting the slabs from the same section of stone keeps the seam quiet. The same guide says quartz patterns can be hard to match across a seam, since many quartz slabs come in one pattern that can't be mirrored.

Solid surface joints are glued with matching adhesive, and DuPont says they can be [glued so they don't stand out](https://www.corian.com/sites/corian.com/IMG/pdf/k-27478_corian_solid_surface_product_overview-2021.pdf). On a solid-surface top, you hunt for the joint.

**Verdict: solid surface. With granite or quartz, ask where the seams will fall before the slab gets cut.**

## Which countertop fits the way you cook

**If you cook big and move fast:** granite. The roasting pan, the knife slip and the hot dish on a holiday all do the least harm on stone. Ask to run the water test on a sample of the slab you like.

**If you want to wipe it and walk away:** quartz. No sealing, no worries about the coffee ring, and a pattern that looks the same from one end of the kitchen to the other. Keep a trivet by the stove and the toaster oven.

**If you plan to stay 20 years and want the top to come back:** solid surface. It needs a trivet and a cutting board, and in return you get joints that don't stand out and a surface you can sand when it scratches.
