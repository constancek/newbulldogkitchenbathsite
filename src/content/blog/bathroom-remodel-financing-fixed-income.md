## Promotional financing on a fixed income

### How does promotional financing work for a retired couple?

With promotional financing, qualified buyers get a new shower for as low as $150 a month, or 12 months no interest, on approved credit. The lender approves the application and sets the terms, and the terms vary from one applicant to the next; Bulldog does not make that call. The appeal for a household on a pension and Social Security is that the shower turns into one more bill on the first of the month, next to the electric and the water, and the savings stay put.

On a fixed income, do the arithmetic on the no-interest plan before you pick it. To finish inside the window you pay the balance divided by the number of months, and that check runs much larger than the payment on a standard plan with a longer term. If it doesn't fit inside the pension and Social Security deposits each month, the no-interest plan only works with savings standing behind it.

### What should you read in a no-interest offer?

Read for two things: whether the interest is deferred, and the date the window closes. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that an offer worded "no interest if paid in full" within a set period is a deferred-interest offer: the lender [tracks interest from the purchase date and adds it to your balance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/how-understand-special-promotional-financing-offers-credit-cards) if any of the promotional balance is left when the period ends. A true zero-interest offer charges interest only on what remains, starting the day the promotion ends.

The same CFPB page warns that the [minimum payment due is usually not enough](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/how-understand-special-promotional-financing-offers-credit-cards) to pay off the balance by the end of the period. In the lender's disclosure, find these four things before you sign:

- Whether the plan is deferred interest or true zero interest.
- The exact date the no-interest window closes.
- The interest rate that applies once it closes.
- The monthly amount that clears the balance before that date. Work it out yourself; don't rely on the minimum printed on the statement.

Then set that amount up as an automatic payment from checking, and write the closing date on the calendar by the phone.

### Will the lender count a pension and Social Security?

Yes. The CFPB says lenders [may not discount or refuse to consider](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/is-a-lender-allowed-to-consider-my-age-or-where-my-income-comes-from-when-deciding-whether-to-give-me-a-loan-en-1181/) income from a pension or other retirement benefit, or from Social Security. A lender can still look at the amount and how likely it is to continue. The cable-bill post goes through the application itself.

## Paying cash for a new shower from savings

### Is paying cash better than financing?

Paying cash costs no interest and leaves no bill behind, and for a couple whose savings run well past what they keep for emergencies, it's often the plain answer. The question is what the money was doing before the shower. A savings account is one thing. Money in a traditional IRA or 401(k) is another, because taking it out has a tax cost.

Whatever account it comes from, leave enough behind for the water heater that quits in January. And if the money sits in a CD that hasn't matured, ask the bank what breaking it early costs. A no-interest plan that runs until the CD comes due may cost less than the penalty, as long as you pay the plan off inside the window.

### What does taking money from an IRA cost at tax time?

The IRS says tax on a traditional IRA is generally deferred until you take a distribution, and that distributions are generally [taxed as ordinary income](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b). A withdrawal big enough to pay for a shower lands on one year's return. The IRS also says [other income is considered](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc423) in working out whether your Social Security benefits are taxable, and that a couple filing jointly adds the spouse's income in. A large one-time withdrawal adds to that other income, so in that year more of your Social Security may become taxable. Take the quote to your tax preparer before you call the brokerage.

## Home equity line of credit on a paid-off house

### How does a HELOC work?

A home equity line of credit, or HELOC, lets you borrow again and again against your equity, which is the value of the house minus what you still owe on it. On a paid-off ranch, the equity is the whole value of the house. The CFPB says you can borrow up to your limit during a [draw period that can last about 10 years](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/), and that HELOCs usually carry a variable rate, so the payment can change from month to month. The same page says [monthly payments are often much higher](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/) once repayment starts at the end of the draw period. Some plans let you lock part or all of the balance at a fixed rate.

### What are the risks of a HELOC for a retiree?

The house is the security. The CFPB puts it in one line: if you fall behind or can't repay on schedule, [you could lose your home](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/). Lenders may charge fees to open the line, and some plans require you to borrow a minimum amount.

For a couple who made the last mortgage payment years ago, putting a lien back on the house for a shower is a real decision. A HELOC makes more sense when you expect to use it for more than the bathroom, the kitchen next spring or a roof, and your bank or credit union offers good terms. Ask them for the rate, the fees and the payment after the draw period ends, and put those next to the plan the consultant shows you.
