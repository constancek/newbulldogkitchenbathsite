## What "as low as $150 a month" means on a remodel quote

The number on the quote is the price of the job. The $150 is a monthly payment on a financing plan, for qualified buyers, on approved credit. The two are related the way a car's sticker and a car payment are related: the payment depends on the price, the plan you pick, and how many months you take.

The low end of our financing runs about $25 a month more than the cable bill, for the shower area gutted to the studs and rebuilt with a new base, solid-surface walls, fixtures and the lifetime warranty. Your own payment may land higher, depending on your quote, your plan and your credit approval, and the consultant will show you the exact figure before you decide.

## No-interest plans vs. 60-month financing plans

Our [financing page](/financing/) lists two kinds of plans, both on approved credit. One is a promotional plan with no interest for a set number of months. The other is a standard plan with terms up to 60 months and a fixed payment that includes interest.

A no-interest plan asks for larger payments over a shorter window and charges nothing extra if you finish inside it. A longer plan spreads the same balance into smaller payments, with interest on top, and that is where a monthly figure as low as $150 comes from, for qualified buyers. A couple on a pension who wants the payment to sit next to the other monthly bills tends to look at the longer plan. A couple with a CD maturing in the spring tends to look at the no-interest window.

The consultant can run both against your quote at the kitchen table. Ask for the monthly figure, the number of months and the total paid at the end for each one, side by side, on paper.

## Deferred interest: the word "if" in a no-interest offer

A no-interest offer tends to read "no interest if paid in full in 12 months," and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau tells shoppers to [read that "if" twice](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/how-understand-special-promotional-financing-offers-credit-cards). Two different plans hide behind that wording.

On a deferred-interest plan, the lender keeps a running tab of the interest from the day of purchase. Pay the balance off inside the window and the lender tears up the tab. Leave any balance at the end, even a small one, and the lender adds the whole tab to your account. On a true zero-percent plan, no tab exists; if a balance remains at the end, interest starts on that remaining amount from that day forward.

The CFPB's example uses a $400 television with $300 paid off inside the window. On a zero-percent plan the shopper owes the remaining $100. On a deferred-interest plan the shopper owes $165, the $100 plus $65 of interest that had been accruing the whole time. The same page warns that the minimum payment printed on the statement is, in most cases, too small to clear the balance before the window closes.

Two habits protect you on either plan. Divide the balance by the number of months in the window, pay that amount every month, and plan to finish a month early. And if the plan runs on a card that carries other balances, ask the lender in writing to apply anything above the minimum to the promotional balance, since the CFPB notes that, without that instruction, extra payments go to the balance with the highest rate.

Ask the consultant which kind our no-interest plan is, and ask for the answer on paper. The terms come from the lender, and the lender's disclosure is the document that governs, so read it before the three days are up.

## Pension and Social Security income count on a credit application

A fear we hear from retired couples is that the application will come back "no" because the paychecks stopped. Federal law is on your side here. Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, a lender [may not discount or refuse to consider](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/is-a-lender-allowed-to-consider-my-age-or-where-my-income-comes-from-when-deciding-whether-to-give-me-a-loan-en-1181/) income from an annuity, a pension or another retirement benefit, from part-time work, or from Social Security. A lender also may not, as a rule, decide on your age alone. A lender may look at the amount of the income and how likely it is to continue, and a pension and Social Security score well on the second test.

"Qualified buyers" means the lender approves the application; Bulldog does not make that call, and the terms vary from one applicant to the next. The application does involve a credit check. FICO, the company behind the score most lenders use, says that for most people [one additional inquiry takes fewer than five points](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/blog/4-surprising-things-that-cause-a-hard-inquiry) off the score.

Some couples would rather pay a fixed monthly amount than pull the money from a 401(k). Part of the reason is that the IRS treats a withdrawal from a traditional 401(k) as income in the year you take it, and the [IRS's own page on 401(k) plans](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc424) points to the rules. That is a conversation for your tax preparer. We are the people who build the shower, and we would rather you ask the question before you sign than after.

## The three-day right to cancel a remodel contract

The Federal Trade Commission's [Cooling-Off Rule](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buyers-remorse-ftcs-cooling-rule-may-help) covers a sale of $25 or more made at your home, including one you invited the salesperson in to make. You have until midnight of the third business day after signing to cancel for a full refund, and you do not have to give a reason. Saturdays count as business days; Sundays and federal holidays do not. The seller is supposed to give you a cancellation form at signing; if you use it, mail it by certified mail and keep a copy, and if you never got one, a dated letter postmarked inside the three days does the same job. Ohio has its own version of the rule, and the [Ohio Attorney General's consumer office](https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/Newsletters/Consumer-Advocate/February-2023/Consumer-Protection-Up-Close) notes that the contractor cannot start work until the three days have passed.

Use the three days. Read the lender's disclosure, and call your bank or credit union to ask what a home-improvement loan would cost from them. Compare the monthly figures and the totals before the third day ends.

Ask the consultant which offer or which plan does more for your project. We would rather you pick the better one than find out at the first statement.

## Six questions to ask before you sign a financing plan

1. Is the no-interest plan deferred interest or true zero percent, and may I see that in the lender's disclosure?
2. On each plan, what monthly amount clears the balance a month before the window closes, and what is the total paid at the end?
3. What is the interest rate after the promotional period ends, and on the longer plan from the start?
4. Who is the lender, and is the application a hard credit pull?
5. Can the $1,250 offer or the 65-and-older discount go with this plan, or do I choose one?
6. If the install date moves, does the first payment move with it?

Write the answers on the back of the quote. If two companies are bidding, ask both the same six and compare the backs of the two quotes before you compare the fronts. Our post on [what sets the price of a one-day shower](/blog/one-day-bathroom-remodel-cost-cincinnati/) covers the front.
