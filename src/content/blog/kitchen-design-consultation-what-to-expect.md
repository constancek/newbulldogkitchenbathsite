You've decided the kitchen needs work. The oak doors have worn through by the handles, the laminate has a burn ring next to the stove, and the corner cabinet is where pot lids go to disappear. Before anyone orders a cabinet door, somebody has to come to the house, measure, and talk it through with you. At Bulldog that visit is the design consultation, and it happens in your kitchen, with the cabinets right there to point at. You'll get more out of it if you know what's coming and have a few things ready.

## What the consultation covers

Bulldog runs every project in four steps: Discover, Design, Build and Enjoy. The consultation is where the first two start.

- **Discover:** we learn about your home, your goals, your priorities and what you have in mind.
- **Design:** we guide the layout, the selections, the cost and the project plan.
- **Build:** your dedicated team coordinates and manages the work, with clear communication and as little disruption as we can manage.
- **Enjoy:** you get your kitchen back and go back to cooking in it.

In practice, a design consultant comes to the house, measures the kitchen, brings samples of colors and finishes, walks you through the options and recommends what fits. The written quote comes after the measuring.

## Five things to have ready before the consultant comes

None of this takes more than an evening. If two of you live in the house, plan for both of you to be home for the visit, since the person who cooks and the person who unloads the dishwasher usually have different complaints.

### 1. A list of what bugs you

Walk through a normal day in the kitchen and write down every snag as it happens. The drawer that sticks. No place to set a hot pan next to the stove. The trash can that lives in the walkway. The cabinet over the fridge nobody can reach.

Be specific. "Nowhere to put the cutting board down by the sink" tells the consultant more than "not enough counter space." Put the list in order, worst first.

### 2. Which cabinets you use most

Open every cabinet and drawer and put a piece of painter's tape on each one: daily, weekly or rarely. Note what sits on the counter because it has no home, and which corner cabinet you've given up on.

That list tells you which organizers are worth paying for. Bulldog installs spice pull-outs, drawer inserts, trash and recycling pull-outs next to the sink, swing-out corner baskets, plate racks and deep drawer organizers. [Seven cabinet organizers for an older kitchen](/blog/kitchen-cabinet-organizers/) goes through which ones earn their space.

### 3. Photos of kitchens you like

Screenshots on your phone, a page torn out of a magazine, a picture of your daughter's kitchen in Mason. They don't need to match each other. Mark what you like in each one: the door style, the counter, the sink, the color of the hardware. A photo of something you don't want helps just as much.

Our [Shop the Room](/inspiration-shop-the-room/) page has kitchen looks to start from. Tell the consultant which one you like, and we recreate it with our materials, sized to your space.

### 4. The appliances you're keeping, with their sizes

If the refrigerator is two years old and staying, the new cabinets have to fit around it. For every appliance you're keeping, write down the model number and measure the width, height and depth: the refrigerator, the range, the dishwasher and any microwave over the range. The space each one needs around it varies by model, and the manufacturer's installation guide for that model number lists it.

If you plan to replace an appliance in the next year or two, say so at the table. It's easier to size an opening for the new one now than to cut a cabinet later.

### 5. A number you're comfortable with, and how you'd pay

You don't have to share it, but knowing it keeps the conversation useful. Decide whether you'd pay outright or use [financing on approved credit](/financing/). Your design consultant goes over the financing options and explains how to apply.

## What happens at the table

### Measuring

The consultant measures the kitchen. Those numbers drive the quote and the order, so it's worth having the room clear enough to reach the walls. Later, before your countertops are made, we digitally template your existing layout for a tight, accurate fit.

### Samples in your own light

The consultant brings samples so you can compare colors and finishes in your kitchen's light, not a store's. A white door that looks crisp under showroom lights can look gray at 4 o'clock in a north-facing Anderson Township kitchen. Hold the samples next to the floor you're keeping and the wall color.

The choices you'll go through:

- **Cabinet doors:** shaker, flat-panel, raised-panel or glass-front, in painted, stained or two-tone finishes.
- **Countertops:** quartz, granite or solid surface, with an edge profile and a sink cutout. [Quartz, granite or solid surface](/blog/quartz-granite-or-solid-surface-countertops/) compares how each holds up over 20 years of cooking.
- **Sinks:** undermount, farmhouse, drop-in or double bowl, with a faucet to match.
- **Backsplash and hardware:** the backsplash, plus the faucet and cabinet hardware in one finish so they match.

### Reface, replace or one piece at a time

The consultant walks you through the options and recommends what fits your kitchen. If the cabinet boxes are solid, refacing puts new doors, drawer fronts and veneer on them. If they're worn or damaged, we recommend full replacement. You can also start with one part (new countertops, a backsplash, a sink and faucet, or organizers) and leave the rest for later.

If you want to know where you stand before the visit, [can my cabinets be refaced?](/blog/can-my-cabinets-be-refaced/) is a five-minute check with a flashlight and a yardstick.

### A look at it in 3D

Before anything is ordered, we can show you your new kitchen in 3D, with the doors, counter and colors you picked. Ask for it. Seeing the whole room at once catches a dark counter on dark cabinets better than two samples on a table.

### Promotions and discounts

Ask about current promotions, and check [Current Promos](/current-promos/) before the visit. Mention the offer when you book, since it has to be presented at the estimate. Promotions can't be combined, and your consultant helps you pick the one that saves you the most. Bulldog also has a discount for homeowners 65 and older and savings for active-duty military and veterans; the consultant has the details.

## After the consultation: the quote, the coordinator, the schedule

When you have the written quote, read it with your list from step 1 next to it. Each item on your list should have an answer in the quote, or a reason it doesn't.

Once you go ahead, you get a dedicated project coordinator who keeps you informed from start to finish. Many kitchen updates take a few days. A full kitchen with new cabinets takes longer, and your project coordinator gives you a detailed schedule before work starts, so you can plan around the work.

Our own crews do the installation, and every installer is background-checked. Every project comes with a lifetime warranty on our materials and workmanship, and if something isn't right, we make it right.

## Questions to ask before the consultant leaves

- Would you reface or replace these cabinets, and why?
- Which promotion applies to this project?
- How many days will we be without a working sink?
- What does the warranty cover, and what do I do if something goes wrong?

[The three questions to ask a contractor before a deposit](/blog/questions-to-ask-bathroom-contractor/) were written about bathrooms, and they work the same way on a kitchen quote.
