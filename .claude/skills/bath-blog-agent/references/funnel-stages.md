# Funnel stages and topic bank

The owner wants the blog to nurture readers at every stage, not only the ones ready to call. Each post belongs to one stage. The stage decides the question, the depth, the amount of Bulldog in the post, and the CTA.

## The four stages

### Awareness: "something is wrong with my bathroom and I'm not sure what to do"

The reader has a problem, not a plan. Gray grout, a tub they struggle with, a soft floor, a bathroom that embarrasses them. They are searching for the problem, not for a remodeler.

- Bulldog content: 10% or less. One mention near the end, as the company that wrote the article.
- Job of the post: explain the problem so well that the reader trusts the author. Teach them one thing they can repeat to their spouse.
- Surround mention: light. Introduce the idea that a bathroom can be built without grout, as one of the options, and move on.
- CTA: another post, or the pricing guide ("if you're curious what this costs in Cincinnati"), or email signup if the owner has a list. Not the phone number alone.
- Length: 900–1,300 words.

### Consideration: "I'm going to do something; what are my options and what do they cost?"

The reader has decided to remodel in the next year. They are comparing tile vs. surround, full remodel vs. tub conversion, franchise vs. local, DIY vs. hire.

- Bulldog content: 25–35%. Bulldog's packages, prices and process appear as concrete examples.
- Job of the post: honest comparison. Give the real tradeoffs. The avatar should finish the post knowing which option fits a person like them, and it should be the surround for most of them.
- Surround mention: central, but fair. Tile gets its due.
- CTA: the pricing guide, the project gallery, or a related decision post.
- Length: 1,200–1,800 words.

### Decision: "I've picked the surround (or nearly); who do I hire and what happens next?"

The reader is choosing between Bulldog and two other bids, or is nervous about pulling the trigger.

- Bulldog content: 50%+. The process, the crew, the warranty, the fixed price, install day.
- Job of the post: remove the last fear. Show them what Tuesday looks like when the crew arrives.
- CTA: book the free in-home estimate, (513) 657-3750, or the contact form.
- Length: 1,000–1,500 words.

### Post-purchase and referral: "we did it; now what?"

The reader is a customer. They want to care for the bathroom, tell a friend, or do the next room.

- Job of the post: care guide, "what to tell your friends," the kitchen or the hall bath next.
- CTA: leave a review, refer a neighbor, book the next room.
- Length: 600–1,000 words.

## The mix rule

Over any run of ten posts: about 4 awareness, 3 consideration, 2 decision, 1 post-purchase. If the last three posts were decision stage, write an awareness post next. Track the stage of each post in the calendar.

## CTA ladder

Use the lowest rung that fits the stage. Each post uses one primary CTA, in the last section, and may reference one more inline.

1. Read a related post (internal link).
2. Join the newsletter (the site has a signup; the best awareness CTA because it keeps the reader without asking for a call).
3. Browse colors and patterns (/inspiration-colors-patterns/) or Shop the Room.
4. See how a one-day install works (/one-day-bathroom-remodel/).
5. Current promos (/current-promos/) and financing (/financing/). Date-check the promo before linking.
6. Schedule the estimate: (513) 657-3750, info@bulldogkitchenbath.com, or /about/#contact.
7. Leave a review or refer a neighbor (post-purchase only).
