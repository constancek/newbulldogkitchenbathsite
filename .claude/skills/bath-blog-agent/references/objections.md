# Objections and concerns

Every consideration and decision post handles at least one of these. Awareness posts may touch one lightly. For each: what the reader says, what they mean underneath, how an installer answers it, and what not to say.

The rule for all of them: concede what is true before you answer. The avatar has been sold to before. A concession buys the next paragraph.

## "Those wall systems look cheap / like a plastic insert / like a motel."

**Underneath:** I've seen the thin, glossy, white tub surrounds from the hardware store and the 1990s. I don't want my daughter-in-law to see that.

**Answer:** Concede that the cheap ones exist and that the reader has probably seen one. Then describe what separates a made-to-order polymer surround: fabricated to the room's measurements, a matte or low-sheen marble pattern instead of high-gloss white, seams at the corners rather than across the wall, trim and a niche or shelf built in. Tell them to judge it from the doorway and then from six inches away, and offer to show a finished one in a ranch like theirs. Photos do the work here; describe two for the owner to add.

**Don't say:** "indistinguishable from real marble." Say "marble-look," "looks like marble from across the room."

## "Isn't tile the real way to do it?"

**Underneath:** I grew up with tile. Tile is what the nice houses have. I'll regret not doing it properly.

**Answer:** Tile is a real way to do it, and a tile contractor will do it well. Bulldog doesn't sell tile, and the post can say so plainly; it reads as honest. For the reader who wants tile's look, point to the tile-look patterns (Subway, Herringbone, Hexagonal) cut into one non-porous panel. Then describe what tile asks of the owner over 20 years in a Cincinnati bathroom: grout that absorbs water and soap, annual sealing most people skip, mildew in the joints, a longer install, a higher price. Describe who should still pick tile (a specific mosaic, a curbless wet room, a hand-set feature wall, a house where they want a design story). Then describe who the surround suits: someone who wants to clean it in ten minutes, who doesn't want to think about it again, who wants the job done in a day. Let the reader place themselves.

**Don't say:** "tile is outdated," "tile always fails."

## "Will it hold up? Will it yellow, crack, scratch?"

**Underneath:** The cultured-marble vanity top yellowed. The fiberglass insert in the rental cracked. I've been burned by "maintenance-free."

**Answer:** Name the materials that gave the reader that experience (thin fiberglass, gel-coat, old cultured marble) and say the polymer is a different material with a different track record. Cite the maker's claims as the maker's claims (resists scratches, chips, stains and fading) and lean on the two warranties, the manufacturer's and Bulldog's lifetime workmanship warranty. Say what to do if something does go wrong: one phone call, one company.

**Don't say:** "indestructible," "will never." Do not invent test data or years-in-service numbers; cite or tag `[CONFIRM WITH OWNER]`.

## "What does it cost, really?"

**Underneath:** Every site says "call for a quote." I assume that means it's expensive and they want me in a room with a salesperson.

**Answer:** Bulldog doesn't publish job prices, so the post can't give the number the reader came for, and it should not pretend otherwise. Say in the second paragraph that the price depends on the measurements and that the quote is written and free. Then earn the read by explaining what sets the price (footprint, base type, door, fixtures, what's under the old tub) so the reader can judge any quote they get. Then the money line: with promotional financing, qualified buyers get a new grout-free, mold-resistant shower for as low as $150 a month, or 12 months no interest, on approved credit. Add the live promos ($1,250 off a full remodel, the 65-and-older discount) with a date check. Never write "affordable" or "budget-friendly"; the monthly figure does that work.

**Don't say:** any dollar figure for the job. Not a range, not "starting at," not "most jobs land around."

**Don't say:** "affordable," "budget-friendly," "cheap." Don't quote a number not on the website.

## "Done in one day? I don't believe it."

**Underneath:** My sister's bathroom took six weeks. Contractors lie about schedules.

**Answer:** Explain why one day is possible for this product and not for tile: the walls and base are fabricated to the room's measurements before the crew arrives, so install day is demo to the studs, plumbing, subfloor, backerboard, panels, fixtures, with no cure time for mortar or grout. Be precise: one day is the wet area, the tub or shower and its walls, gutted and rebuilt. The measurement visit comes before; floor, vanity and toilet are a separate conversation and add time. Walk through install day hour by hour in the decision-stage post.

**Don't say:** "one day" for the whole bathroom.

## "What's under my tub? Will the price go up once the wall is open?"

**Underneath:** I know there's something wrong down there. I'm scared of the change order.

**Answer:** Say what the crews usually find in a 1960s–90s Cincinnati bathroom (confirm the list with the owner: wet subfloor, mold on the drywall behind tile, galvanized or corroded plumbing, no vapor barrier). Then say how Bulldog handles it. The old site listed mold remediation, subfloor replacement and new plumbing as included with a fixed price; the new site is silent, so tag `[CONFIRM WITH OWNER]` and ask. If it is still true, it is Bulldog's strongest answer in the whole blog; use it in several posts.

## "I don't want strangers in my house for weeks."

**Underneath:** I'm 70 and I live alone. / I work from home. / The dog.

**Answer:** One day. One crew, in-house, the same people the reader met at the estimate. A named Project Manager with a cell number. Dust barriers, floor protection, cleaning before they leave. Describe what the hallway looks like at 5pm.

## "Grab bars and walk-in showers are for old people. We're not there yet."

**Underneath:** I don't want the bathroom to say "nursing home" to me every morning.

**Answer:** Don't argue with the feeling. Reframe around the house, not the person: a low threshold is easier for the grandkids, for carrying laundry, for anyone with a bad knee for a month. Describe grab bars that look like towel bars and a built-in seat that reads as a shelf. Say the choice is theirs and it's easier to build in now than add in ten years.

**Don't say:** "seniors," "elderly," "as you age," "golden years."

## "Will it hurt resale? Do buyers want tile?"

**Underneath (Dave and Karen):** I don't want an inspector or a buyer's agent to flag it.

**Answer:** Buyers and inspectors flag water damage, soft floors, mildew and dated tile, not clean grout-free showers. A new non-porous shower removes a line item from the inspection. Cite a public source if you make any claim about remodel ROI (search for current Cost vs. Value data for the Cincinnati region and cite it). The warranty runs for as long as the current owner holds the home, so do not sell it as a transferable asset; say the buyer gets a shower with nothing to regrout.

## "Why not a franchise one-day bath company?"

**Underneath:** The big names advertise on TV. They must be safer.

**Answer:** Don't name them. Describe what to ask any one-day bath company: who installs (employees or subcontractors), is the price fixed before work starts, what's included when the wall is opened, who holds the warranty, can you see a finished job nearby. Then answer those questions for Bulldog. The reader draws the comparison.

## "Can we stay in the house? Can we use the other bathroom?"

**Answer:** Yes to staying. Water is off to that bathroom during the day; confirm with the owner whether whole-house water is off and for how long. The second bathroom stays usable. If the house has one bathroom (Margaret), describe how the crew sequences the toilet so it's usable by evening (confirm).

## "What if something goes wrong in five years?"

**Answer:** One call to Bulldog. Lifetime warranty on product and workmanship for as long as they own the home, registered by the crew before they leave on install day. Confirm with the owner what the first step is (photo by text to the PM? a site visit?) and describe it concretely.

## Handling order in a post

In a consideration post, address objections in the order the avatar raises them: price, looks, durability, timeline, what's under the tub, disruption, warranty. In a decision post, start with disruption and install day, because the reader has already accepted price and looks.
