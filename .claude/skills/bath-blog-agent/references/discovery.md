# Discovery

Two discovery processes live here. The first runs once, with the owner, to make the avatar and content plan real. The second runs for every post and takes two minutes.

## 1. Owner discovery (first session)

Ask in batches of three to five. Use AskUserQuestion when the session has it, with the likely answers as options and "Other" available. Questions marked **must** come first. Stop when you have enough to rewrite the avatar; you can always ask more later.

### About the customers (must)

1. **must** Think of your last five one-day bath customers. Age, town, type of house, and who made the call (husband, wife, adult child)?
2. **must** What did they say on the first phone call? Give me the actual words if you remember them.
3. **must** What did they believe about "those wall systems" before they saw yours, and what changed their mind?
4. **must** What objection do you hear most in the home, and what do you say back?
5. Who are the customers you turn away or lose, and why? (Price shoppers, people set on tile, out-of-area?)
6. How many of them came to you already wanting tile and left with the surround? What flipped them?
7. Do adult children ever drive the purchase for a parent? How often?
8. What do customers say six months later? Any phrases you hear more than once?

### About the product (must)

9. **must** What is the surround made of, in the words you use with a customer? Is "acrylic" or "polymer" or something else right?
10. **must** No grout. Is there caulk anywhere (corners, base, around the valve)? Can I say "nothing to reseal"?
11. **must** Which colors and patterns do you offer, and which two or three do most customers pick?
12. **must** Walk me through install day hour by hour. When can they shower?
13. What does the lifetime warranty cover and exclude? Does it transfer if they sell the house?
14. What cleaners do you tell them to use and avoid?
15. Is the one-day product available for tub-shower combos and full-bath gut jobs, or only tub-to-shower conversions? What happens to the floor, vanity and toilet in a "gut"?
16. What do you find behind the old tile most often (rot, mold, old plumbing)? How do you handle it, and does the price change?

### About the business

17. How long has Bulldog been in business, roughly how many baths a year, and can I use those numbers?
18. Financing: who is the partner, typical terms, can I mention them?
19. Which featured projects can I describe in detail, and do you have before/after photos and customer permission for each?
20. Do you have Google reviews I can quote? Paste a few.
21. Which towns do you most want more work in?
22. Who writes the posts' byline? Owner's name, "the Bulldog team," or a named project manager?

### About tile (important for positioning)

23. (Answered Oct 2026: no tile anymore; one-day wet-area gut; $10K–$16K; site testimonials are placeholders.) Follow-ups: what moves a job from $10K toward $16K? Is the quote fixed after signing? What's included when the wall is open?
23a. Do you have real Google or Facebook reviews I can quote, with permission?
24. What breaks on tile showers you get called to replace? Age of the shower when it fails?

### About the blog

25. Where does the blog live and who publishes it? Any word-count, image, or formatting rules from the site builder?
26. Do you have an email list? If yes, each post can end with a signup instead of the phone number for early-stage readers.
27. Do you want posts to link to the pricing guide form, the project gallery, or the contact page? All three exist on the site.
28. Any words or phrases you never want to see (competitor names, "cheap," "seniors")?
29. Any posts or pages already written that I should read first for voice?

### How to run it

- Open by showing the draft avatar in six or seven lines and asking "how close is this?" The owner will correct it faster than they will fill in a blank.
- After each batch, reflect one line back ("so most calls come from the wife, and the husband's complaint is the tub wall") so the owner can fix misreadings.
- Save answers verbatim in a `discovery-notes.md` section of the avatar doc. The owner's phrasing is the best raw material for post titles.
- Finish by producing the first 12-post calendar (see `funnel-stages.md`) and the rewritten avatar.

## 2. Per-post discovery (every post)

Fill `templates/content-brief.md`. If the owner gave only a topic, answer these yourself from the avatar and the funnel map, and show the owner the first three answers in two or three lines before drafting.

1. Stage: awareness, consideration, decision, or post-purchase?
2. The one question this post answers, in the words the reader would type.
3. Which avatar variant is asking, and what is going on in their house that made them search?
4. What do they believe right now that this post needs to correct or complete?
5. What are they afraid this post will try to sell them? (Name it so the post can defuse it early.)
6. What concern from `objections.md` must the post address?
7. What proof do we have? (A fact from `brand-and-product.md`, a cited public source, an owner-confirmed project.)
8. Which CTA matches this stage?
9. Two internal links (other posts or site pages) this post should point to.
10. One thing an installer would say about this topic that a marketer would not.

Question 10 is where the posts stop sounding like every other remodeler's blog. Ask the owner for these over time and keep a running list in the avatar doc.
