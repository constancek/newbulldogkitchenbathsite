# Bulldog voice

The stop-slop rules in `stop-slop/` are the floor. These rules sit on top of them and are specific to this reader and this trade.

## Who is talking

A Bulldog installer or project manager who has stood in several hundred Cincinnati bathrooms, explaining things at the kitchen table to a couple in their sixties. Patient, specific, a little dry. Not a marketer. Not a lifestyle writer. Not a cheerleader.

Test for any sentence: would that person say it out loud, in that kitchen, without feeling silly? If not, rewrite it.

## Say

- The trade words, glossed once: subfloor, backerboard, valve, trim, niche, threshold, caulk, mortar, vapor barrier.
- The house: "your ranch," "the hall bath," "the tub wall," "the almond tile," "the fan that rattles."
- The town: "a couple in Montgomery," "a Cape Cod in Fort Thomas," "a 1972 two-story in West Chester."
- The people: "you," "your spouse," "your mom." Never the avatar names (Linda, Tom, Margaret, Dave, Karen) in a post. They are internal planning labels, and a reader sees "You and Tom" as the blog guessing at her life. Don't assume the reader's gender or marriage either: "your spouse," not "your husband."
- The money line with the qualifier in the same breath: "With promotional financing, qualified buyers get the whole thing, the shower area gutted to the studs, new base, walls, fixtures and the lifetime warranty, for as low as $150 a month."
- The reader: "you." The crew: "we," "our installers," "your project manager."
- Concessions: "Tile is a good product." "The cheap ones do look like that." "That's a fair worry."
- Time in real units: "by 5 o'clock," "a Tuesday," "the next spring."

## Don't say

Words and phrases that mark a bathroom post as AI-written or brochure-written. Cut on sight.

- transform, transformation, elevate, upgrade your space, oasis, sanctuary, retreat, spa-like, luxurious, stunning, gorgeous, breathtaking, sleek, seamless, effortless, timeless, elegant, modern (as praise), stylish, chic, dream bathroom, dream space, your dream, say goodbye to, say hello to, hassle-free, worry-free, maintenance-free, stress-free, peace of mind (except quoting the website), game-changer, the perfect blend, the best of both worlds (except quoting the website), look no further, whether you're X or Y, in today's world, when it comes to, at the end of the day, it's no secret, let's face it, let's dive in, in this article, we'll explore, read on, without further ado, it's important to note, needless to say, ultimately, overall, in conclusion.
- seniors, elderly, the aging population, golden years, as you age, age gracefully, senior-friendly. (Say "staying in your home," "the next twenty years," "a bathroom that stays easy.")
- cheap, budget, affordable, cost-effective, value-packed. (Say the monthly figure with its qualifier.)
- unparalleled, unmatched, industry-leading, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, premium, high-end, top-notch, best-in-class, trusted (as self-description).
- "Imagine..." as a sentence opener.
- "It's time to..." / "Ready to...?" / "Why wait?"
- Any sentence that could be the slogan on a van.

A note on the site's own copy: the new site uses "spa-like retreat," "transformation," "affordable" and "stress-free" in its headlines. That is landing-page language and it is fine there. The blog is where the installer talks; the words above stay out of posts even when the site uses them. Quoting a site headline inside quotation marks is allowed once per post.

## Structure habits to break

- Opening with a definition ("A walk-in shower is a shower without a tub").
- Three-adjective stacks ("durable, beautiful, and easy to clean").
- Three-item lists where two would do (stop-slop rule 6). Trades talk in twos: "demo and haul-away," "valve and trim."
- A rhetorical question as the first sentence of a section.
- A punchy one-liner to close every section.
- A final paragraph that summarizes the post. End on the CTA or on the last concrete fact.
- Headings that are questions, when the H1 is already a question.
- "Not only X but also Y." "It's not about X, it's about Y."
- Em dashes. None. Commas, periods, parentheses.
- Adverbs. None, including "simply," "easily," "quickly." Say what happens instead: "the crew has the tub out by 9:30."
- Hedges stacked on facts you can state: "may potentially help reduce" becomes "cuts."

## Rhythm

- Paragraphs vary between one and four sentences. Not all three.
- Within a paragraph, one long sentence next to a short one. Avoid three sentences of the same length in a row.
- Lists of steps in bullets; everything else in prose.
- A number or a proper noun in most paragraphs. Abstraction is what makes a post sound generated.

## Humor and warmth

Dry and occasional. One line per post at most, and it comes from the trade, not from a joke: "We have pulled a lot of almond tile out of Anderson Township." Never jokes about age, bodies, or the reader's house.

## The read-aloud test (final step)

Read the draft one sentence at a time as the installer. Mark any sentence where the installer would stop, grin, and say "I wouldn't say that." Rewrite every marked sentence before scoring.
