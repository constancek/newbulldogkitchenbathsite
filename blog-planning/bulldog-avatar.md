## Primary avatar: Linda and Tom

**Who they are.** Linda is 62, Tom is 64. They have lived in a 1968 brick ranch in Anderson Township for 26 years. Tom retired from GE Aviation two years ago; Linda works three days a week at a dental office and plans to stop next year. Two grown kids, one in Columbus, one in Mason with the grandkids. Household income in retirement is comfortable but fixed: pension, Social Security, a 401(k) they do not want to draw down for a bathroom.

**The house.** Paid off. Roof done in 2019, HVAC in 2021. The hall bath and the master bath are both original: 4-inch square tile in almond, a cast-iron tub with a sliding glass door, a one-piece vanity with a cultured marble top, a fan that rattles. Linda has regrouted the master shower twice. The grout goes gray again within a year. There is a soft spot in the floor by the tub she does not want to think about.

**What they want.** A bathroom that looks like it belongs in this decade, that they can clean in ten minutes on a Saturday, and that they will not have to touch again. Linda wants it to look nice when her daughter-in-law visits. Tom wants to stop stepping over a 16-inch tub wall and wants a bar to hold.

**What they are afraid of.**
- A contractor who takes a deposit and disappears, or who starts the job and stretches it across six weeks with the house torn up. Linda's sister in Loveland had that happen.
- Being upsold. They have sat through a two-hour "one-day bath" sales pitch in their living room before and felt cornered.
- A result that looks "like a plastic insert" or "like a nursing home."
- Spending $15,000 and hating it.
- Change orders. The number going up once the wall is open.
- Having strangers in the house for weeks. Dust in the hallway. Using the hall bath for a month.
- Looking foolish for not knowing how bathrooms are built.

**What they believe going in.**
- A "real" remodel means tile. Acrylic and "those wall panels" are what you get at a franchise or an apartment.
- A bathroom remodel takes a month and costs $25,000 or more.
- Grout is just something you live with.
- Walk-in tubs and grab bars are for "old people," and they are not there yet.

**How they research.** Linda does most of it, on an iPad in the evening and on her phone at the dentist's office between patients. Google first ("how long does a bathroom remodel take," "best way to clean shower grout," "walk-in shower cost Cincinnati"). Then she reads reviews, then she asks her sister and the neighbor who had work done. She does not watch TikTok. She will watch a two-minute YouTube video if a post links one. She reads the whole article if the first paragraph is useful, and she leaves if it sounds like a brochure.

**What moves her.** Specifics from houses like hers. A straight answer about price. Photos of a finished bathroom in a ranch, not a mansion. Someone explaining why her grout keeps going gray. A company that puts its process on the website instead of hiding it behind a sales call.

**What she will say when she calls.** "We have an older house in Anderson and the master shower is original. We'd like to get rid of the tub and have something easier to keep clean. We're not sure if we want tile or one of those systems. What do you recommend?"

**Objections she will raise, in order.** Price. Will it look cheap. Will it hold up. Will you really be done in a day. What about the floor under the tub. Can we stay in the house. What happens if something goes wrong later.

**How she describes success six months later.** "We should have done this ten years ago. I spray it down after Tom showers and that's it."

## Secondary variant A: Margaret, widowed, planning ahead

71, Hyde Park, a 1920s Tudor she and her late husband bought in 1990. One bathroom upstairs with a clawfoot tub she can no longer get in and out of safely. Her son in Oakley is pushing her to "make the house work for the next ten years." She does not want to move. Lead concern is safety without the bathroom looking medical. Price is secondary; disruption is primary (she lives alone and does not want a crew in the house for weeks). She will call because her son sent her a link.

Writes to this reader: aging-in-place posts, low-threshold shower posts, "what to do about a clawfoot tub," anything on staying in an older home.

## Secondary variant B: Dave and Karen, selling in two to three years

56 and 55, West Chester, a 1994 two-story. Kids gone, planning to downsize to Mason or Florence in a few years. The master bath has a builder-grade tub-shower combo with a cultured-marble surround that has yellowed. They want it to show well and to not become a line item in a buyer's inspection report, but they do not want to spend tile money on a house they are leaving. Lead concern is return on money and timeline. Will shop three bids.

Writes to this reader: cost comparison posts, resale posts, "what buyers notice in a bathroom," "tile vs. surround for a house you'll sell."

## Who is not the avatar

- Flippers and landlords. They buy on price alone and would be a different blog.
- Homeowners under 45 remodeling for style. They want tile, a freestanding tub and a design story. Bulldog serves them with its tile tiers; the blog does not chase them.
- Anyone outside the Cincinnati / Northern Kentucky service area.

## How to use the avatar when writing

Before drafting, answer in one line each:

1. Which variant is reading this post?
2. What did they type into Google?
3. What do they believe right now that is wrong or incomplete?
4. What are they afraid the article is going to try to sell them?
5. What would make them send it to their spouse?

Write the post for the person those five answers describe.
